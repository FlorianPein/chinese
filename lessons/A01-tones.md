# A01: The four tones + neutral tone

> **Goal:** recognise and say the four tones and the neutral tone, using 13 common words you'll need straight away.
>
> **Listen:** [🔊 all words and sentences](https://florianpein.github.io/chinese/#listen/A01) · **Time:** ~15 min · **Data:** [`data/A01.json`](../data/A01.json) · **Next:** [U01-L01: Hello](U01-L01-hello.md)

Every Mandarin syllable has a **tone**: a pitch shape. The tone is part of the word, just like its vowels and consonants. Change the tone and you often get a different word:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **mǎi**<br><sub>mai3</sub> | to buy | low, dipping |
| **mài**<br><sub>mai4</sub> | to sell | sharp fall |

## The tones

Think of your voice range as 5 levels, from **1 (low)** to **5 (high)**.

| Tone | Mark | Pitch | Feels like (English) | Feels like (German) |
|------|------|-------|----------------------|---------------------|
| **1st** | ā | high and flat: 5 → 5 | holding a high note, like *"Aaah"* at the doctor's | a held, sung *"Laaa"* |
| **2nd** | á | rising: 3 → 5 | a surprised *"What?"* | a surprised *"Was?"*, or *"Ja?"* answering the phone |
| **3rd** | ǎ | low, dips down: 2 → 1 (→ 4) | a low, doubtful *"Hmm…"* | a low, reluctant *"Na ja…"* |
| **4th** | à | sharp fall: 5 → 1 | a firm *"No!"* or *"Stop!"* | a firm *"Nein!"* or *"Doch!"* |
| **neutral** | a | short, light, no mark | the unstressed *-er* in *butter* | the unstressed *-e* in *bitte* |

These comparisons are only approximate. The Chinese tones are steadier and more deliberate.

**About the 3rd tone:** in normal speech it's mostly just **low** (2 → 1). The rise at the end (→ 4) only shows up when the word is said on its own or with emphasis. Beginners usually make it too bouncy, so think *"low and relaxed"*, not *"down-up"*.

**Hand trick:** draw the tone shape in the air with your hand while you say the word. It feels silly but works.

## Your first sentences

You can already build real sentences with these words:

| Pinyin | English | Literally |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| **Tā shì shéi?**<br><sub>Ta1 shi4 shei2?</sub> | Who is he / she? | he / is / who |
| **Nǐ shì shéi?**<br><sub>Ni3 shi4 shei2?</sub> | Who are you? | you / are / who |
| **Duì!**<br><sub>Dui4!</sub> | Right! / Correct! | right |
| **Shì ma?**<br><sub>Shi4 ma5?</sub> | Really? / Is that so? | is + question |

Notice that the question word **shéi** sits where the answer would go: *Tā shì shéi?* → *Tā shì māma.* (She is mum.) Chinese doesn't move question words to the front.

## Pronunciation tip: same sound, different word

These pairs differ only in tone. Say each pair slowly, drawing the tones with your hand:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **mǎi** · to buy | **mài** · to sell | 3rd vs 4th |
| **wèn** · to ask | **wěn** · to kiss | 4th vs 3rd |
| **shuìjiào** · to sleep | **shuǐjiǎo** · dumplings | 4-4 vs 3-3 |

The classic tongue-twister uses all five tones:

> **Māma mà mǎ ma?**
> <sub>Ma1ma5 ma4 ma3 ma5?</sub>
> Is mum scolding the horse? (mum / scold / horse / question)

## Practice

1. **Name the tone** (1, 2, 3, 4 or 5 for neutral):
   a) hē · b) rén · c) wǒ · d) duì · e) ma · f) tā · g) nín · h) shì
2. **Numbers → marks.** Rewrite with tone marks:
   a) ni3 · b) ta1 · c) shei2 · d) dui4 · e) ma1ma5
3. **Which one?**
   a) You're at a market and want to *buy* something: **mǎi** or **mài**?
   b) Which one do you eat: **shuìjiào** or **shuǐjiǎo**?
   c) You want to *ask* someone something: **wèn** or **wěn**? (Choose carefully!)
4. **Build a question:** how would you ask *"Who is mum?"*

## Say it aloud

Read each line 3 times, slowly, drawing the tones with your hand. Then say them at normal speed.

1. **Nǐ shì shéi?**
2. **Tā shì shéi?**
3. **Shì ma?**
4. **Duì!**
5. **Māma mà mǎ ma?**
