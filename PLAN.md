# Learning Plan: Mandarin through Pinyin

**Learner:** total beginner; explanations in English, pronunciation compared to English *and* German
**Goal:** talking to people: introducing yourself, small talk, friends, hobbies, plans, feelings
**Script:** Pinyin only for now (no characters)
**Time:** around 10–15 min on a typical day, but flexible: skip days, or do several lessons at once

### Decisions

| Topic | Decision |
|-------|----------|
| Pinyin notation | Tone marks (`nǐ hǎo`) are the main form. In the early lessons (Track A + Units 1–3) tone numbers are shown small underneath (<sub>ni3 hao3</sub>), then dropped. Neutral tone = `5`. |
| Your answers | Either form is accepted everywhere (`nǐ hǎo`, `ni3 hao3`, `ni3hao3`), in exercises, flashcards and chat. |
| Sound comparisons | English *and* German in Track A, and in the pronunciation tips of each lesson |
| Characters | Stored in the data, hidden everywhere for now |
| Flashcard page | Published with GitHub Pages so it works on your phone |
| Personal details | None. Self-introduction lessons use example people plus "swap in your own" word lists (countries, cities, jobs…) |

## 1. Principles

1. **Phrases first, words inside them.** Every lesson is built around whole phrases you can say straight away. Vocabulary and grammar are pulled *out of* those phrases rather than taught in isolation.
2. **Tones from day one.** Pinyin is always written with tone marks (`nǐ hǎo`, with tone numbers as a small helper at first) and you always hear it. A wrong tone can change the word (`mǎi` buy vs `mài` sell), so tones are never optional.
3. **Small units.** One lesson is one sitting of about 10–15 minutes. On a busy day do one, on a free day do three, on a bad day do nothing. The review system catches up whatever you missed.
4. **Review beats new material.** Flashcards use spaced repetition: cards you know come back rarely, cards you miss come back soon. If you've been away, start with review rather than a new lesson.
5. **Speak out loud.** Every lesson ends with something to say aloud, and chat sessions with me give you conversation practice.

## 2. The two tracks, mixed together

There are two kinds of lessons: **sound lessons (A)** and **conversation lessons (B)**. They alternate, so pronunciation is never dry theory:

- **Sound lessons teach each sound through common words**, not nonsense syllables. The words come from the conversation lessons just before and just after: some you've already met, some are a preview of the next lesson. So every A lesson also builds real vocabulary, and every B lesson starts with words you can already pronounce.
- **Conversation lessons point back to the sounds.** Each one has a pronunciation tip about the sound most likely to go wrong in its phrases, linking to the A lesson where it was taught.

### Track A: sounds, taught through common words

| # | Topic | Key content | Example words used |
|---|-------|-------------|--------------------|
| A01 | The four tones + neutral tone | Pitch shapes, hearing and saying each tone | `māma` mum, `shéi` who, `nǐ` you, `shì` to be, `ma` (question) |
| A02 | Tone pairs | 2-syllable combinations (most words have 2 syllables) | `xièxie`, `zàijiàn`, `lǎoshī`, `péngyou`, `míngzi` |
| A03 | Easy initials | b p m f d t n l g k h; `b` vs `p` (unaspirated vs aspirated) | `bù`, `péngyou`, `duì`, `tā`, `gēge`, `kàn`, `hěn` |
| A04 | Simple finals | a o e i u ü; `e` isn't English *e* | `wǒ`, `hē`, `è` (hungry), `nǚ`, `qù` |
| A05 | Tricky initials 1 | j q x (tongue down behind lower teeth) | `jiào`, `qǐng`, `xièxie`, `xǐhuan`, `jiā` |
| A06 | Tricky initials 2 | zh ch sh r vs z c s | `shì`, `Zhōngguó`, `chī`, `rén`, `zàijiàn`, `cài` |
| A07 | Compound & nasal finals | ai ei ao ou an en ang eng ong; `-n` vs `-ng` | `hǎo`, `méi`, `yǒu`, `rén`, `Yīngguó`, `fàn` |
| A08 | Spelling rules | y/w, ü written u after j/q/x, `iu` = iou, `ui` = uei, `un` = uen | `yǒu`, `wǒ`, `jiǔ`, `duì`, `qù` |
| A09 | Tone changes (sandhi) | 3rd + 3rd → 2nd + 3rd, `bù` → `bú`, `yī` changes | `nǐ hǎo`, `hěn hǎo`, `bú shì`, `bú kèqi`, `yíxià` |

Each sound lesson comes with **English and German sound-alikes**, common mistakes, and minimal pairs to train your ear. German helps a lot here, because several Pinyin sounds are closer to German than to English:

- `ü` = German *ü* (`nǚ` ≈ *nü*), which English doesn't have
- `z` = German *z* in *Zeit* (*ts*), not English *z*
- `h` = rough like German *ch* in *Bach*, not soft English *h*
- `x` ≈ German *ch* in *ich*, but with the tongue pressed against the lower teeth
- `ou` ≈ English *go*: here English is the better match

Each sound uses whichever language gives the closer match, and sometimes both.

### Track B: Conversation units (the main course)

Each unit has about 4–6 short lessons, then a **dialogue** lesson that brings the whole unit together, then a **review** lesson.

| Unit | Theme | You'll be able to… | Grammar met along the way |
|------|-------|--------------------|---------------------------|
| 1 | Hello & politeness | greet, thank, apologise, say goodbye | — |
| 2 | Who are you? | give a name, nationality, where someone lives (example people + word lists to swap in your own) | `shì` (to be), `jiào` (to be called), questions with `ma` |
| 3 | Questions | ask *what / who / where / how*, and ask back with *"and you?"* | `shénme`, `shéi`, `nǎr`, `ne` |
| 4 | Numbers & age | count, give your age and phone number | numbers, `duō dà` (how old) |
| 5 | Family & friends | talk about family members and friends | `yǒu` / `méiyǒu`, measure word `gè` |
| 6 | Work & study | say what you do and where you work or study | `zài` (at), `zuò` (do) |
| 7 | Likes & hobbies | say what you like doing, ask others | `xǐhuan` + verb, `huì` (can), `hěn` |
| 8 | Time & days | days, times, *today / tomorrow / weekend* | time words before the verb |
| 9 | Making plans | invite someone, accept or decline, arrange to meet | `yào`, `xiǎng`, `ba` (suggestion) |
| 10 | Eating & drinking together | order, say what you'd like, toast, compliment food | `hē`, `chī`, `yìdiǎnr` |
| 11 | Feelings & opinions | how are you *really*, tired, happy, *I think…* | `juéde`, `tài … le` |
| 12 | What happened? | talk about the weekend, things you did | `le`, `guo`, `méi` |
| 13 | Keeping a conversation going | fillers, *sorry, again please*, *speak slowly* | `qǐng`, `màn yìdiǎnr` |

**Rough timeline at ~1 lesson/day:** all sound lessons + Units 1–4 in ~4 weeks, all 13 units in ~3–4 months. This is a guide, not a deadline.

## 3. What a lesson looks like

Every lesson file follows the same template so it's quick to work through:

1. **Goal**: one line, e.g. *"Say your name and ask someone else's."*
2. **Phrases** (about 5): Pinyin (tone numbers small underneath in early lessons), English, a literal word-by-word breakdown, and audio.
   ```
   Nǐ jiào shénme míngzi?        What's your name?
   ni3 jiao4 shen2me5 ming2zi5
   (you / be-called / what / name)
   ```
3. **New words**: pulled from the phrases, with tone marks, meaning and audio.
4. **Pattern box**: the one grammar idea of the lesson, shown with 2–3 examples.
5. **Pronunciation tip**: the one sound or tone in this lesson most likely to trip you up.
6. **Practice**: 3–5 small exercises (translate, fill the gap, pick the right tone, reorder words).
7. **Say it aloud**: a short line to record or say out loud.
8. **Answer key**: at the bottom, folded away.

## 4. Tools in this repository

| Tool | What it is | How you use it |
|------|------------|----------------|
| **Lesson files** (`lessons/`) | Markdown, one file per lesson | Read on GitHub or locally, then do the exercises |
| **Phrase data** (`data/*.json`, one file per lesson) | Every phrase and word of a lesson: Pinyin, English, literal gloss, and *characters, hidden for now*. Tone numbers are generated from the tone marks, so they can't disagree. | Audio and flashcards are generated from this; a check script makes sure each lesson file matches its data |
| **Audio** (`docs/audio/`) | One MP3 per phrase/word, generated by text-to-speech with a native Mandarin voice | Click from the lesson or flashcards; slowed-down versions for new material |
| **Flashcard page** (`docs/`, served by GitHub Pages) | A single web page: flashcards (Pinyin → English, English → Pinyin, audio → meaning), tone quizzes, spaced repetition; answers accepted with tone marks or numbers; progress saved in your browser | Open it daily, on laptop or phone; it tells you how many cards are due |
| **Progress log** (`PROGRESS.md`) | What you've done, and which sounds and phrases you struggle with | I update it after our chat sessions and use it to plan the next lesson |
| **Chat sessions** | You open a session and say e.g. *"quiz me on Unit 2"* or *"role-play meeting someone at a party"* | I quiz, correct your Pinyin and tones, and adapt the next lessons |

Characters are stored in the data from the start, so if you decide to learn them later we can switch them on without redoing anything.

## 5. Your routine (flexible)

| Day type | What to do |
|----------|------------|
| **Normal day (10–15 min)** | Flashcard review (~5 min), then 1 new lesson |
| **Busy day (2–5 min)** | Flashcard review only, which still counts |
| **Free day (30 min+)** | Review, 2–3 lessons, or a chat role-play with me |
| **Back after a break** | Review only until the due count is low, then continue |

Rule of thumb: **don't start a new lesson if more than ~40 flashcards are due.** Clear the review first.

---

## 6. Milestones

- **After Track A + Unit 1:** read any Pinyin aloud with roughly the right sounds and tones.
- **After Unit 4:** hold a 1-minute self-introduction (name, where from, where you live, age, job).
- **After Unit 9:** have a short back-and-forth: ask about someone, talk hobbies, make a plan.
- **After Unit 13:** have a 5-minute simple conversation with me in Pinyin, with mistakes allowed.

Each milestone has a short "checkpoint" chat session where I test you on it.

## 7. Build order

1. Set up the repo: `README.md`, `PROGRESS.md`, data format, lesson template.
2. Write **A01 (tones)** and **U01-L01 (hello)** so you can start straight away.
3. Build the audio generator and add audio to those first lessons.
4. Build the flashcard page and publish it with GitHub Pages.
5. Continue lessons, roughly a week of content ahead of where you are, adjusted by what `PROGRESS.md` shows.
