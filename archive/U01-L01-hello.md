# U01-L01: Hello, thank you, goodbye

> **Goal:** greet someone (casually or politely), say thank you, answer a thank-you, and say goodbye.
>
> **Listen:** [🔊 all phrases and words](https://florianpein.github.io/chinese/#listen/U01-L01) · **Time:** ~10–15 min · **Data:** [`data/U01-L01.json`](U01-L01.json) · **Before:** [A01: Tones](A01-tones.md) · **Next:** A02: Tone pairs

## Phrases

| Pinyin | English | Literally |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| **Nǐ hǎo!**<br><sub>Ni3 hao3!</sub> | Hello! / Hi! | you / good |
| **Nín hǎo!**<br><sub>Nin2 hao3!</sub> | Hello! (polite: older people, strangers, customers) | you (polite) / good |
| **Xièxie!**<br><sub>Xie4xie5!</sub> | Thank you! | thank-thank |
| **Bú kèqi.**<br><sub>Bu2 ke4qi5.</sub> | You're welcome. | not / polite, as in "no need to be so polite" |
| **Zàijiàn!**<br><sub>Zai4jian4!</sub> | Goodbye! | again / see, like German *Auf Wiedersehen* |

## Pattern: [person] + hǎo = hello

Chinese greets *someone*: you say who you're greeting, then **hǎo**. There's no word for "is" here. Adjectives like **hǎo** work on their own.

| Pinyin | English |
|--------|---------|
| **Nǐ hǎo!**<br><sub>Ni3 hao3!</sub> | Hello! (to one person) |
| **Nǐmen hǎo!**<br><sub>Ni3men5 hao3!</sub> | Hello! (to a group) |
| **Dàjiā hǎo!**<br><sub>Da4jia1 hao3!</sub> | Hello, everybody! |
| **Lǎoshī hǎo!**<br><sub>Lao3shi1 hao3!</sub> | Hello, teacher! |

**In real life:** *Nǐ hǎo* is right for meeting someone new. Friends who already know each other often skip it and just say the other person's name, or a casual *"Hāi!"* (from English *hi*).

## Pronunciation tip: nǐ hǎo is said "ní hǎo"

When two **3rd tones** meet, the first one turns into a **2nd tone**. It's still *written* `nǐ hǎo`, but *said* **ní hǎo** (rising, then low). Try it the "wrong" way with two low dips and you'll feel why: it's awkward to say. This is covered properly in A09; for now just remember **nǐ hǎo = ní hǎo**.

The same thing happens with **bù**: before another 4th tone it becomes **bú**. That's why you see **bú kèqi** (kè is a 4th tone). Textbooks usually write this change; Pinyin dictionaries don't.

**Two sounds to watch** (both covered properly in A05 and A06):

- **z** in *zàijiàn* is like the *ds* in English *kids*, or a soft German *z* in *Zeit*. It's not an English *z*.
- **x** in *xièxie*: put your tongue tip behind your lower front teeth and say *"see"*. It sounds between English *s* and *sh*, close to the German *ch* in *ich*.

## Practice

1. **Match** the Pinyin with the English:
   a) Zàijiàn · b) Xièxie · c) Nín hǎo · d) Bú kèqi · e) Nǐmen hǎo
   1) Thank you · 2) Hello (polite) · 3) You're welcome · 4) Goodbye · 5) Hello, all of you
2. **What do you say?**
   a) You meet a friend's grandmother.
   b) Someone holds the door for you.
   c) Someone thanks you.
   d) You leave a party.
   e) You walk into a room with several people.
3. **Tone numbers.** Write these with numbers: a) hǎo · b) nín · c) zàijiàn · d) xièxie · e) kèqi
4. **Literally?** What do these mean word by word? a) Zàijiàn · b) Nǐ hǎo
5. **Sound check.** a) How is *Nǐ hǎo* actually pronounced? b) Why is it **bú** kèqi and not **bù** kèqi?

## Say it aloud

Play both parts, out loud, 3 times. The 2nd time, try it at normal speed.

> **A:** Nǐ hǎo!
> **B:** Nǐ hǎo!
> *(A gives B something)*
> **B:** Xièxie!
> **A:** Bú kèqi.
> **A:** Zàijiàn!
> **B:** Zàijiàn!
